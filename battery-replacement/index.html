A car that clicks, cranks slowly, or does nothing at all when you turn the key usually has a battery problem, but not always. Oilrun comes to your home or workplace in Pensacola or Pace, tests the battery and the charging system, and replaces the battery right there if that's what it needs. We're not a roadside service, so we work on vehicles parked at a home, an apartment, or a job site rather than on the side of the road.

Signs the battery is on its way out

  • Slow, labored cranking, especially first thing in the morning.
  • A single click or rapid clicking when you turn the key.
  • Dim headlights or interior lights with the engine off.
  • Dash warning lights that flicker, or a radio and clock that keep losing their settings.
  • A battery that's more than a few years old. Florida heat is hard on batteries.

What we do on-site

We test the battery's state of charge and its ability to deliver cranking amps, then test the alternator output and check for a parasitic drain. That step matters: a new battery won't fix a weak alternator or a short that drains the car overnight. If the battery is the problem, we install the correct group size and terminal type for your vehicle, clean the cable ends, and secure the hold-down. On vehicles that need it, we register the new battery with the car's computer so the charging system treats it correctly. We take the old battery with us for recycling.

FAQ

Common questions

Is it the battery or the alternator?

A battery that dies overnight or after sitting is usually the battery. A car that starts with a jump but dies while driving, or shows a battery light while driving, points to the alternator. We test both before replacing anything.

Do you bring the battery with you?

Yes. Tell us your year, make, and model, or send a photo of the label on the current battery, and we'll bring the right one.

Can I supply my own battery?

Yes. Customer-supplied parts aren't covered by our warranty, and if a customer-supplied battery fails, labor to replace it is charged again.
